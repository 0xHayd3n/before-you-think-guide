# 1. Concepts and architecture

[Guide home](../README.md) · [Next: three frameworks](02-three-frameworks.md)

## The question behind the framework

The paper asks how AI changes both **the processes by which people think** and **the information from which they think**. A search summary, a recommendation feed, or an increasingly familiar writing assistant can do both at once. It can perform a task while also deciding what becomes salient. The authors argue that treating these as completely separate psychological and epistemic effects misses their interaction. [Introduction and §2, pp. 1–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=1)

The dual-process starting point is familiar: System 1 labels fast, intuitive processing; System 2 labels slower, deliberative processing. The paper acknowledges the reductionism of this division. System 0 is another theoretical description, not a newly discovered brain structure or a neuroscience measurement. Its numeral emphasizes that AI can shape inputs before either intuition or deliberation becomes engaged.

## Three levels, one proposed relationship

Table 1 combines three levels that should be kept distinct in analysis even though the authors argue they can coexist in practice.

| Level | What is being claimed | Named property | What a reader would need to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Computational | A data-driven system learns from user behavior, adapts, and structures inputs | Anticipatory personalization | How outputs depend on the individual and change through feedback |
| Psychological | A resource becomes habitually trusted and woven into a person's cognitive routines | Adaptive invisibility | Whether it is experienced and used as an integrated component, rather than as an occasional outside adviser |
| Epistemic | Filtering and framing determine what information and options become available for thought | Automation of relevance judgment | Which items are promoted, excluded, or framed before the user explicitly asks a question |

This is an original condensed explanation of [§2.1 and Table 1, pp. 4–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=4), not a replacement for the table.

A personalized output alone addresses the computational level. A user finding it useful alone does not establish the psychological claim. A platform ranking information addresses the epistemic level without automatically proving constitutive integration. A fully realized instance, on the paper's account, combines all three; existing systems approximate them to varying degrees.

## What is the boundary of System 0?

The authors reject equating System 0 with a particular device or application. Its proposed boundary follows a person's actual interactions across relevant AI systems. It also does not include every computation a platform might perform: available time, skill, access, and the current situation limit the information with which the person can interact. The resulting perimeter is variable and user-dependent. [§2.1, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=5)

This matters when evaluating a claim such as “this phone is my System 0.” The relevant unit is a relationship among a person, tasks, information, and technical supports. Hardware ownership cannot establish that relationship. Nor does the paper say every AI encounter qualifies.

## How the extended mind argument works

The extended mind tradition allows cognitive processes to include resources outside the biological brain. A familiar example is information kept in a trusted notebook. The paper moves from the **parity** idea—an external resource can perform an internal-like function—to a **complementarity** approach, where different internal and external resources contribute in different ways. [§2.2, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=6)

Its account of integration draws on dimensions associated with Heersmink: information flow, reliability, durability, trust, transparency, individualization, and transformation. The paper argues that AI can satisfy several at once. That is a philosophical inference from patterns of use; the present paper does not operationalize a composite score or validate a cutoff separating “tool” from “part of cognition.”

There is a useful ambiguity to watch. Transparency can mean that a tool is readily usable and disappears into a routine; it can also mean that its operation is understandable and inspectable. An interface can become effortless while its governing objectives remain opaque. That possibility is central to the later colonization argument.

## Examples and their evidential status

Navigation tools are used to illustrate reliance and possible loss of unaided wayfinding. Writing assistance illustrates the blurred attribution of language and authorship. These passages interpret existing studies through the System 0 lens; they are not new tests of integration. [§2.3, pp. 7–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=7)

Wearables supply a further illustration: an algorithm's interpretation of bodily data might become the person's own answer to “how do I feel?” The authors explicitly mark this as **more speculative**. A device reporting stress is not, by itself, evidence that interoception has been replaced or that an individual has been cognitively colonized. [§2.3, pp. 8–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=8)

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning in this guide |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-reflective | Operating before explicit conscious evaluation; not necessarily inaccessible forever |
| Constitutive integration | Being a component of a cognitive process, rather than only an external cause affecting it |
| Causal embedding | Being part of the environment that influences cognition without being counted as part of the cognitive system |
| Epistemic | Concerning knowledge, belief, attention, relevance, or justification |
| Cognitive offloading | Using external resources to reduce or relocate task demands |
| Anticipatory personalization | Adapting information and options to an individual in advance of an explicit request |
| Adaptive invisibility | Integration that makes mediation less noticeable in ordinary use |
| Reflective endorsement | The person's considered approval of the relevant shaping or goals, not simply a click or habitual acceptance |
| Cognitive colonization | The paper's proposed profile of opaque, externally directed integration that fails reflective endorsement |

Read [module 4](04-cognitive-colonization.md) before treating the final term as a claim about any actual case.
