## What kind of evidence is present?

The paper offers a literature-based conceptual argument. It does not recruit participants, introduce a new experiment, release a new dataset, perform a systematic meta-analysis, or validate a diagnostic instrument. Table 2 relates selected existing findings to the authors' interpretation of System 0. It also includes overlap with competing explanations and a speculative wearable example. [§2.3, pp. 7–9; Table 2, pp. 16–17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=16)

The descriptions below are **the paper's characterizations of cited literature**. The underlying studies were not independently audited for this companion. They should be read directly before making numerical, causal, clinical, or population-wide claims.

## From observation to interpretation

The reasoning often has this form: a study observes a behavior, the authors read it as a marker of integration, then they connect integration with externally directed influence. Each transition adds a claim.

For example, a person retaining an AI-induced bias after the system is removed would support **carryover**. To conclude **cognitive colonization**, one would still need to address integration, the direction and origin of the relevant objectives, opacity, and reflective non-endorsement. The paper's proposed diagnostic is joint, not a rule that any persistent effect counts. [§5, pp. 18–22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=18)

Similarly, convergent outputs can be observable without deciding whether the cause is internal incorporation, shared prompts, the model's suggestion distribution, or a common social environment. The System 0 reading may be productive, but the observation alone does not select it.

## Breakdown as a research strategy

Footnote 7 proposes learning from disruption: a support's role can become visible when it is withdrawn. Relevant signs could include impaired performance, disorientation, or surprise at one's dependence. The paper links this to infrastructural inversion. [§4, footnote 7, p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=15)

Withdrawal is a possible method, not an experiment executed in this paper. A well-designed study would need comparison groups, baseline skill, exposure history, a reason for expecting a particular decrement, and measures that distinguish temporary inconvenience from a changed cognitive process. It would also need to avoid inferring misalignment from dependence alone.

## Bibliographic and source-access cautions

The reference list occupies pp. 26–32. Some in-text dates and names differ from the list, and several table citations are not supplied there in a complete matching form. For instance, Branda is discussed as 2025 but listed as 2026; Morgan and Feldman likewise appear with different years; “Large” in §3 appears alongside a reference listed as Lange. These are source-level inconsistencies, not discrepancies repaired silently by this guide. [§§3–4, pp. 11–13; references, pp. 26–32](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=26)

Where the PDF does not give a complete unambiguous citation, this companion does not invent a DOI or reconstruct a study's methods. Use the table as a route into a source audit. The [claim index](../metadata/claims.json) therefore labels literature mappings as interpretations of cited evidence and does not encode them as new experimental results.
