## Compare the level of explanation before comparing the labels

System 0, System 3, and Thinkframes do not simply name three interchangeable pieces of a single model. The paper compares different ways of locating AI's influence: an adviser consulted during a reasoning episode, an environment shaping collective interpretation, or a personalized layer incorporated into an individual's thinking. The comparison itself is the authors' argument. [§§3–4, pp. 10–17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=10)

| Framework as characterized in this paper | Main unit | Characteristic relation | Main concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tri-System Theory / System 3 | Individual reasoning episode | The person consults an external source and may adopt its answer | Cognitive surrender |
| Thinkframes | Shared cognitive and informational ecology | The person operates within systems that organize attention and interpretation | Convergence and homogenization |
| System 0 | Individual architecture, with population consequences | A personalized, habitual, pre-reflective layer becomes integrated | Cognitive colonization under additional conditions |

This comparison preserves how **these authors characterize** the alternatives; it does not establish that the alternatives could never be extended to cover other cases.

## The argument about System 3

Shaw and Nave's Tri-System Theory describes an external artificial reasoning process alongside intuition and deliberation. The paper credits its experiments with documenting strong adoption of AI answers, including faulty ones, and recognizes the value of the trust and individual-difference findings. These are experiments in another paper, not experiments conducted here. [§3, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=10)

The authors raise three main objections.

1. **Novelty of deference.** Accepting an AI answer without independently reconstructing it may resemble ordinary deference to a doctor or colleague. The authors ask whether speed and availability establish a new kind of cognition or intensify a familiar social process.
2. **The consultation boundary.** A task in which someone can choose to consult a chatbot may miss ambient curation that precedes the recognition of a question. System 0 is intended to target that earlier influence.
3. **Inside versus outside.** If surrender means deference to an external authority, the authors question the simultaneous description of that authority as a system of one's own mind.

These are arguments from [§3, pp. 11–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=11). The first includes a suggested human-adviser comparison; this paper does not run that comparison. A reader should not turn the authors' expectation into a finding that human and AI advice have identical effects.

A textual caution: p. 10 contains wording about surrender that conflicts with the surrounding account of uncritical adoption. This guide follows the consistent definition across the paper's abstract, introduction, and critique; it does not use that isolated sentence to redefine the term. For a detailed account of the experiments, consult Shaw and Nave directly.

## The argument about Thinkframes

The paper treats Thinkframes as AI-mediated environments that coordinate information, interpretation, and collective attention. Examples include recommendation algorithms, moderation, and interface design. It acknowledges the importance of collective cognition: people think together, inherit knowledge, and coordinate through shared environments. [§4, pp. 13–14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=13)

The objection is that an ecological description can leave out what is specifically incorporated into each person. A shared recommendation system can tailor outputs to each user, while each user comes to experience the resulting organization of information as an ordinary part of thinking. The authors contend that a theory centered on the environment does not adequately explain that phenomenological and architectural integration. [§4, pp. 14–15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=14)

This does not mean collective effects disappear. The authors instead propose an aggregation story: individually calibrated systems share training distributions, design incentives, or objectives; many individual trajectories can then converge. Whether this is the best explanation of a particular collective effect remains an empirical and theoretical question.

## What the paper claims System 0 adds

The central claim is that a single architecture can explain both an individual's integration and a population pattern. On this account, a person's AI-mediated relevance judgments generate effects that System 3 captures at the level of deference and Thinkframes captures at the level of collective ecology. The authors call System 0 the generative mechanism. [§4, p. 15; conclusion, pp. 24–26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13658v1#page=15)

That is an explanatory ambition, not an experimentally identified mediator. Overlap is also acknowledged in Table 2: automation bias is not distinctive to System 0; vocabulary convergence can fit more than one framework; the comfort-growth concern is partly shared.

## A useful reading discipline

When the paper says an alternative “cannot explain” a phenomenon, separate three questions:

- Does the alternative's **published formulation** address it?
- Could the alternative be **extended** to address it without losing its core commitments?
- Does a proposed **observation distinguish** the alternative from System 0?

Only the third becomes a discriminating empirical test once the predictions, measurements, and relevant alternatives are specified. [Module 7](07-questions-and-research-agenda.md) sketches ways to make that distinction concrete.
